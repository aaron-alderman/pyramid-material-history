## Purpose and conclusion

**The pyramids are the canonical example**, with the Great Pyramid of Khufu as the first named anchor and other monuments retained as separately identified comparisons. Their public familiarity gives readers a reason to enter the map; their materials, construction, internal structure and surviving context give the connected disciplines concrete questions to answer. Attention is an editorial reason for choosing the example. Evidence determines which explanations it supports.

A pyramid brings the full material history into view: where its stone came from, how its constituents behave, how people worked and moved it, how the structure was assembled, what later exposure changed, and what instruments can recover today. A worked stone is simultaneously an electronic material, an assemblage of minerals, a participant in chemical and mechanical processes, and a surviving historical record. Its hardness, chemical composition, surface shape and archaeological setting constrain different parts of that account.

The missing middle is a **material history**: a sequence of transformations under specified conditions, followed by preservation and observation. A useful explanation predicts both how material was changed and what evidence that process would leave. The corpus can organize this work through its existing algebra of typed objects, transformations, assumptions, losses and evidence.

The route begins with the corpus's admission–dynamics–detection programme and established quantum and continuum physics. It passes through orbitals, bonds, periodic and nonperiodic matter, lattice motion, chemistry, transport, minerals and Earth processes. It reaches archaeology through manufacture, use, deposition, preservation, measurement and historical inference. These are connected scales and questions, with feedback between them. They do not form a universal derivation of archaeology from one symmetry group.

The canonical example therefore supports several productive journeys. A reader can begin with a visible monument, follow a material question down to mineral structure, orbitals or a reaction pathway, and return through predicted traces to archaeological evidence. A calculation should descend only as far as the chosen question requires; no whole-pyramid quantum computation is implied.

Granite–alkali treatment is one nested candidate investigation under quarrying and working. Its experimental and archaeological gates remain explicit in the case below. Other pyramid materials and construction questions retain equal access to the same substrate.

## What MECE means here

Chemistry, chemical physics, geology and geophysics overlap. Treating their names as mutually exclusive boxes would erase the bridges being sought. This atlas instead partitions **atomic work items by their declared output**, while retaining disciplines as overlapping annotations.

| Operation family | Exclusive primary output | Boundary rule |
|---|---|---|
| O1 Constraint | A specified admissible domain | States what is allowed, without selecting a solution |
| O2 Equivalence | An invertible representation map on a declared domain | A truncation or discarded observable belongs to O3 |
| O3 Reduction | An effective model and its loss ledger | Includes marginalization, coarse graining, truncation and homogenization |
| O4 State solution | A predicted state, spectrum or static response for fixed conditions | A trajectory or rate belongs to O5 |
| O5 Evolution | A predicted trajectory, rate, flux or material transformation | Includes reactions, transport, fracture, growth and preservation |
| O6 Intervention | A specified preparation or control protocol | Its predicted consequences are separate O4 or O5 items |
| O7 Measurement | An instrument record with calibration and uncertainty | Reconstructed properties or histories belong to O8 |
| O8 Inference | An estimated property, model or history from records | Its evidence disposition is a separate O9 item |
| O9 Assessment | A bounded support, challenge, qualification or unresolved decision | Does not act as a physical evolution law |

One item has one primary output. A sentence that both changes basis and discards bands must become an O2 item followed by an O3 item. A laboratory operation and an inference about ancient practice become O6/O7 and O8/O9 items. Parallel coupled processes may share a carrier without becoming the same operation.

This partition is exhaustive for the declared scientific workflow by construction, not an exhaustive catalogue of every scientific mechanism. An unclassifiable item must remain in the explicit residual queue and trigger a schema review. The machine register includes unclassified work and unresolved bridges; their presence prevents a false claim of scientific exhaustion.

### Composition and coupled inputs

Use sequential composition for compatible ports, parallel composition for jointly available processes, a joint input for a mechanism requiring several conditions, alternatives for competing mechanisms, and feedback with an explicit state and delay. The [relationship algebra](https://github.com/aaron-alderman/spin23-relational-geometry/blob/main/corpus/programmes/dynatology/brain_relationship_algebra_v1.md) provides an existing operational precedent for these distinctions; transporting its notation does not transport its biological claims.

For instance,

\[
(\text{mineral assemblage},\text{reagent activities},
\text{temperature history},\text{exposed area},\text{transport})
\longrightarrow \text{alteration history}
\]

is a joint-input relation. Reaching the mineral node alone does not satisfy the other inputs. A proposed positive interaction between heating and chemistry is a separate claim requiring an interaction test.

The [Sourcery ALE algebra](https://github.com/aaron-alderman/spin23-relational-geometry/blob/main/corpus/programmes/frontier/wave-7/frontier_wave7_43_sourcery_ale_algebra.md) retains an ordered path and accumulated assumptions, losses and evidence. For composable paths,

\[
\nu(pq)=\big(A(p)\cup A(q),L(p)\cup L(q),E(p)\cup E(q)\big).
\]

The order of operations remains in the path even when the accumulated sets agree. Heating before applying a reagent and applying it before heating may therefore be different histories. A loss cannot disappear merely because a later explanation sounds plausible. A failed bridge remains a refusal or unresolved record outside admitted scientific composition.

### Records and physical quantities

A proposal record can carry dimensionless metadata while its subject carries electron-volts, joules per mole, seconds, pascals or cubic metres. [AZ-083](https://github.com/aaron-alderman/spin23-relational-geometry/blob/main/corpus/programmes/admissibility/admissibility_83_first_common_algebra2_path_and_legacy_dimension_projection.md) and [AZ-085](https://github.com/aaron-alderman/spin23-relational-geometry/blob/main/corpus/programmes/admissibility/admissibility_85_physical_admittance_operator_and_held_out_current_witness.md) make this distinction operational.

For this atlas, **no bridge is promoted to an admitted quantity-level transformation by its presence in the register**. Such promotion requires exact endpoint signatures, physical units and normalization, a declared approximation or map, an uncertainty account, evidence and a discriminator. A dimension delta alone cannot certify a multi-input physical equation: the inputs, constants and dimensional homogeneity of the equation must also be checked.

## Symmetry breaking and the other kinds of passage

The Claimipedia response atlas uses parent symmetry → breaking operation → residual symmetry → response consequence, followed by force, carrier, observable, source of energy, dissipation and null model. Its inspected source identity is retained in the [source bindings](../corpus/sources.json). This is a useful template when the relation actually asserts a physical symmetry change.

Seven separate passage kinds are retained. They are annotations on atomic assertions, not a claim that a real experiment can undergo only one of them.

| Passage kind | What must be demonstrated | Example and information at risk |
|---|---|---|
| Exact representation | An invertible map on the declared space preserves the stated quantities | Complete Bloch and Wannier descriptions of the same retained band subspace; an additional band truncation is separately recorded |
| Explicit physical symmetry reduction | The Hamiltonian, boundary or forcing loses a specified symmetry | A surface removes bulk translation normal to it; terminations and surface conditions still need physical models |
| State symmetry reduction | A selected state has less symmetry than its governing law or reference ensemble | A ferroelectric domain selects a polarization; free-energy landscape, stability and preparation determine realization |
| Information reduction | A many-to-one map discards distinctions | Homogenizing grains into a bulk response can erase reactive grain boundaries |
| Open-system passage | A subsystem exchanges matter, energy or information with its surroundings | Chemical reservoirs sustain flux; their work and entropy ledger must remain explicit |
| Evidential selection | Records alter support for candidate models or histories | Similar tool marks narrow a candidate set only to the extent their formation and preservation are discriminating |
| Domain translation | A supplied dictionary connects differently typed descriptions | Electronic structure informs a chemical model only through specified energies, operators, states and conditions |

An edge asserting several passages must be factored into atomic steps or publish an ordered compound description. A change of vocabulary by itself is none of these transformations.

For genuine symmetry claims, retain the parent action \(G\), intervention or order parameter, residual stabilizer \(H\), and the predicted degeneracy, tensor constraint or selection rule. If \(G\), \(H\) or the representation is unknown, record that unknown. "Lower symmetry" is not a numerical prediction of a rate, hardness or conversion efficiency.

### Physical symmetry descent through the material scales

The relevant symmetry belongs to a declared law, reference structure, prepared state or statistical ensemble. These owners must remain separate. A model with rotationally invariant laws can contain a crystal state that selects preferred directions.

| Level | Parent and retained structure | Difference maker | Consequence that can be derived |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isolated atomic reference | A spherical atomic Hamiltonian can carry spatial rotation symmetry, with spin and spin–orbit structure treated explicitly | External fields, nuclear structure or the selected approximation change the operator | Angular-momentum sectors, degeneracies and transition selection rules within that model |
| Molecule or coordination site | The geometry's point group replaces the isolated spherical reference for the electronic problem | Multiple nuclear centres and their arrangement | Orbital multiplets can split; matrix elements depend on representations and the transition operator |
| Periodic crystal | A specified space group combines lattice translations and compatible spatial operations; it need not be a direct product | Repeated basis atoms, composition and order | Bloch labels, symmetry-related wave vectors and allowed tensor components |
| Crystal surface | Retain only operations preserving the actual surface and termination | A boundary removes the bulk environment on one side | Normal translation is lost; surface-localized states and different reaction sites can occur |
| Defect or grain boundary | Retain operations preserving the defect configuration or its declared ensemble | Missing atoms, substitutions, dislocations or mismatched grains | Degeneracies, localization and response constraints can change; their magnitudes require dynamics |
| Ordered material domain | The selected state's stabilizer can be smaller than the reference law's symmetry | An order parameter and its energetic, thermal and preparation conditions | Domain-dependent polarization, magnetic or elastic responses when allowed by the residual group |
| Liquid glass or disordered solid | Instantaneous configuration symmetry and statistical ensemble symmetry differ | Structural disorder, averaging and observation timescale | Average isotropy may coexist with locally distinct reactive sites |
| Reactive interface | The full system retains its physical accounting while subsystem conditions become asymmetric | Chemical reservoirs, gradients, stresses and oriented surfaces | Directed flux and anisotropic rates can be allowed; rate laws and energy sources still have to be supplied |
| Rock or sediment fabric | Grain orientations and spatial texture can reduce statistical isotropy of an effective medium | Growth, deformation, deposition, fracture and alteration | Anisotropic elastic, electrical, transport or dissolution response, if constitutive laws and homogenization justify it |
| Worked and preserved object | A process and later exposure select a particular spatial and temporal history | Human operations, use, burial and environmental overprinting | Distinguishing marks and altered layers may survive; historical attribution requires inference |

Two useful algebraic checks make these statements concrete. If an operator \(O\) and states transform under a declared group, a transition amplitude \(\langle f|O|i\rangle\) must respect the corresponding representation selection rules. An allowed representation channel still needs a nonzero matrix element and available final states; [AZ-127](https://github.com/aaron-alderman/spin23-relational-geometry/blob/main/corpus/programmes/admissibility/admissibility_127_transition_operators_selection_rules_and_lifetime_frontier.md) develops that gate.

For a constitutive tensor \(L\) mapping generalized forces to responses, invariance under a retained operation \(g\) requires

\[
D_{\rm out}(g)L=L D_{\rm in}(g).
\]

Polar and axial quantities require their own transformation rules. Restricting from \(G\) to a subgroup \(H\) can enlarge the allowed coefficient space because fewer constraints remain. It does not require any newly allowed coefficient to be large or nonzero. The corpus's [lithium-niobate workbench](https://github.com/aaron-alderman/spin23-relational-geometry/blob/main/corpus/programmes/admissibility/admissibility_148_lithium_niobate_as_a_relational_physics_workbench.md) supplies a concrete material example of symmetry, domains, couplings and calibrated channels.

Broken detailed balance in a driven reaction model is also distinct from fundamental violation of microscopic time reversal. A preferred manufacturing sequence and an evidential preference among historical explanations are different kinds of asymmetry again. They must not be assigned a physical residual group without an actual group action.

## Orbitals Bloch states and computation

This is the central bridge between microscopic physics and chemistry of real materials.

An orbital is a one-electron state in a declared description. Atomic labels, molecular orbitals, localized bonding functions, Kohn–Sham orbitals and Bloch states have different roles. Their plotted shapes do not by themselves specify a reaction trajectory or a measured electron path.

For a periodic one-electron reference Hamiltonian,

\[
\psi_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)
=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r).
\]

Bloch organization uses the crystal's translation symmetry. It must be distinguished from the Bloch sphere used to represent a two-level density matrix. A rock containing many minerals and grain boundaries has no single global Bloch band structure. Its constituent crystalline phases can have useful periodic reference calculations.

For a chosen band subspace, a unitary gauge choice and Fourier transform connect extended Bloch functions with localized Wannier functions. This can connect band structure to a local bonding description. Smoothness and localization require their own hypotheses; topology can obstruct a desired global gauge. Dropping bands or fitting a reduced hopping model creates a separate approximation and loss ledger. [Marzari and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.5411).

The computational route has several independent gates:

1. Specify electrons, nuclei, geometry, charge, spin and surroundings.
2. Choose the physical approximation: for example a Born–Oppenheimer potential surface, density functional or correlated electronic method.
3. Choose numerical representation and boundary conditions: local basis, plane waves, pseudopotentials, supercell, slab or cluster.
4. Solve and establish convergence in basis, cell size, sampling and solver tolerances.
5. Obtain forces, barriers, response or spectra with the assumptions needed for that observable.
6. Compare with an independently measured or withheld quantity and propagate remaining model uncertainty.

Quantum ESPRESSO's PWscf provides a concrete plane-wave/pseudopotential DFT implementation; this identifies a calculation family, not a guarantee of reaction-rate accuracy. [PWscf documentation](https://www.quantum-espresso.org/Doc/user_guide_PDF/pw_user_guide.pdf).

Force constants connect energy derivatives to a mass-weighted dynamical matrix and phonon frequencies. The harmonic calculation supplies local vibrational information around a reference structure. Finite-temperature reactions, melting and irreversible fracture can require anharmonicity, evolving structure and larger-scale models. [Phonopy formulation](https://phonopy.github.io/phonopy/formulation.html).

Classical electronic-structure computation and computation on quantum hardware are alternative computational strategies. A quantum algorithm must still supply an encoded Hamiltonian, state preparation, error and resource estimates, and an observable. Changing the computing hardware does not close the model-to-material bridge.

## Chemistry to geology and geophysics

Chemical thermodynamics constrains possible phase assemblages and reaction directions. Kinetics determines rates and accessible pathways. Transport controls which reactants reach which surfaces and where products accumulate. Mechanical response controls fracture and grain release. Their coupling can change accessible area, porosity and flow, which then changes subsequent chemistry.

For a specified species basis,

\[
\dot{\mathbf c}=S\mathbf j+\mathbf b,
\]

where \(S\) is the stoichiometric matrix, \(\mathbf j\) contains reaction fluxes and \(\mathbf b\) represents exchange or transport in consistent units. A conserved elemental row \(\ell\) obeys \(\ell^TS=0\). A reaction free energy is \(\Delta_rG=\sum_i\nu_i\mu_i\), and an equilibrium condition does not determine the relaxation rate. Open-network cycles require a reservoir and dissipation account. [Open reaction-network thermodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.1181), [time-dependent driving and free energy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07257).

USGS PHREEQC provides an established interface among aqueous speciation, mineral equilibrium, surface processes, kinetic reactions, transport and inverse geochemical calculation. A calculation must retain its database and activity-model range. An aqueous model cannot be assumed valid for a concentrated molten hydroxide system. [PHREEQC introduction](https://water.usgs.gov/water-resources/software/PHREEQC/documentation/phreeqc3-html/phreeqc3-2.htm).

Geology then adds assemblages and histories: two rocks with similar bulk oxide totals can differ in mineral phases, grain size, fractures, alteration and provenance. USGS illustrates the composition distinction with a granite containing 70% bulk SiO₂ but 22% quartz. [USGS mineral and bulk composition](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/components-igneous-rocks).

Geophysics adds spatially distributed response and inversion. Velocity, conductivity, magnetic response, gravity or thermal observations constrain combinations of geometry and material properties. Multiple observations can reduce ambiguity, but shared calibration and geological assumptions must be retained. Exact-data nonuniqueness can exist even in simple geophysical models. [USGS research on geophysical nonuniqueness](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/inverse-problem-refraction-travel-times-part-i-types-geophysical-nonuniqueness-through).

## From geology to archaeological inference

The full historical passage is

\[
\text{available materials and resources}
\to\text{ordered human operations}
\to\text{product and waste}
\to\text{use and repair}
\to\text{deposition and preservation}
\to\text{sample and detector record}
\to\text{comparison of histories}.
\]

Each arrow can erase distinctions. Recycling can remove workshop waste. Weathering can alter residues. Cleaning can remove a surface layer. Sampling can miss a localized feature. Several mechanisms can therefore reach similar present appearances, and a single original mechanism can leave different records in different environments.

Tool marks need a reference collection, formation mechanism, scale and preservation model. Reflectance Transformation Imaging combined with tools, experiments and craft expertise is an existing archaeological approach. [Serotta's primary study](https://doi.org/10.29353/rime.2023.5098).

Chronology is another typed bridge. Date of geological formation, date of manufacture, date of use, date of burial and date of a later repair are different targets. Dating a sample constrains the event linked to the relevant clock and contextual association. Oxford's OxCal combines calibrated age information with chronological models; the model's associations and stratigraphic assumptions remain substantive inputs. [Oxford OxCal](https://c14new.arch.ox.ac.uk/oxcal.html).

For an explicit probabilistic comparison,

\[
p(h\mid y,c)\propto p(y\mid h,c)\,p(h\mid c),
\]

where \(h\) is a candidate history, \(y\) the observed records and \(c\) the archaeological and measurement context. A forward model from history to surviving record supplies the likelihood. A qualitative comparison can be used when justified probabilities are unavailable, with its decision rule stated. Repeated descriptions of the same observation are not independent confirmations.

## Granite and alkali treatment within the pyramid case

### Separate the claims

The user-supplied FoMaHun post reports treatment of granite with sodium carbonate and with hydroxides, including a claimed approximately 168°C treatment using a NaOH–KOH mixture. It is a source report here; its photograph, product identity, temperatures, durations and removal rates have not been independently established by this atlas. Its relevance to a particular pyramid component requires a separate material, provenance, chronological and process bridge. Quarry marks at Aswan are comparison evidence, not observations of treatment in a pyramid.

The initial claims must be separated:

1. A specified reagent reacts with specified mineral phases under specified conditions.
2. The treatment changes strength, grain release or material-removal rate.
3. A subsequent operation creates a specified surface texture.
4. The texture and associated chemistry can survive a specified history.
5. Comparable traces occur in a securely contextualized ancient object or quarry.
6. Available resources and process performance make the proposed ancient operation plausible.
7. The joint evidence discriminates that operation from alternatives.

The first three concern mechanism and engineering. The last four concern preservation and history. They require different evidence.

### Retain the actual chemistry

The simple carbonate reaction

\[
\mathrm{SiO_2+Na_2CO_3\to Na_2SiO_3+CO_2}
\]

is a representative silicate-forming reaction. Industrial soluble silicates have composition-dependent silica-to-alkali ratios, and waterglass refers to soluble-silicate solutions. The industrial association describes both furnace and hydrothermal routes. A treated granite surface still requires product identification and a mass balance. [Soluble-silicate manufacture](https://www.cees-silicates.org/index.php/manufacture).

A NaOH–KOH mixture can have a melting point near 170°C. That phase property does not specify the rate of granite alteration, and hydroxide availability is a different historical question from availability of carbonate-rich natron. [Molten-salt phase-behaviour study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ces.2024.120433).

The approximately 851°C melting point associated with pure sodium carbonate is not a universal reaction-onset threshold. A controlled quartz/carbonate study reported appreciable reaction below fusion, with strong dependence on temperature, composition and time. Its powder-mixture results do not supply a bulk granite-removal rate. [Howarth, Maskill and Turner](https://sgt.org/store/viewproduct.aspx?id=15875694).

Granite and wood ash also require different material identities. Similar oxide-analysis labels do not imply matching phases, solubilities or reactivity. Natural natron and commercial washing soda need composition and hydration declarations rather than an automatic identity with pure anhydrous Na₂CO₃.

### Compare mechanisms and combinations

The physical comparison should include untreated and mechanically worked controls, heat without reactive alkali, reactive treatment without mechanical work, and combined sequences. These factors are experimental design variables, not preparation instructions. Temperature, duration, water content, transport, reagent composition, contact geometry and stone mineralogy remain recorded covariates.

The discriminating outputs are material loss and elemental balance; dissolved or solid reaction products; altered-layer depth; mineral-selective attack; crack patterns; change in mechanical removal; surface topography; and cost in time, reagent and energy. A weakened intergranular layer followed by grain release is a different mechanism from dissolving most of the rock. Both may produce material removable by a soft implement afterward.

For an explicitly defined response \(Y\), a factorial interaction contrast such as

\[
\Delta_{AB}=Y_{AB}-Y_A-Y_B+Y_0
\]

tests departure from additivity on that chosen response scale. Its uncertainty and baseline must be stated. A positive contrast alone does not identify the microscopic coupling; it identifies where a coupled model is needed.

### Connect the laboratory to the ancient record

Yi's 2026 paper explicitly investigates molten sodium carbonate and granite cracking, with preliminary experiments and an archaeological interpretation. It provides a relevant candidate and comparison source. It does not validate the post's lower-temperature hydroxide report or establish a unique explanation of ancient surfaces. [Primary study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02315-y).

Mechanical comparators remain active. A photogrammetric pounding experiment measured removed volume and found a slower rate than earlier expectations, motivating further investigation of quarrying methods. That result neither establishes chemical assistance nor excludes it. [2023 experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.daach.2023.e00284).

The proposed archaeological decision should use a joint signature: worked morphology, subsurface mineral alteration, residues or their plausible disappearance, tool and waste evidence, resource and equipment feasibility, chronology and site context. A matching outline alone has low discriminatory power. If a proposed process cannot produce the observed subsurface structure under plausible conditions, that bridge fails even if its surface looks convincing.

## Coverage boundaries and retained residuals

The domain scope covers inorganic and organic materials, metals, ceramics, glasses, stone, pigments, binders, water, soils, biological alteration and relevant human operations through the same typed interfaces. Listing these classes does not provide their reaction laws or historical dossiers. The pyramids are the canonical example; granite and alkali treatment is a nested candidate investigation. Each additional material and process requires its own instantiated laws and historical dossier.

Astrophysical production of elements, nuclear reactions, relativistic electronic corrections, strong correlation, quantum nuclear effects and biological agency enter when the chosen carrier and measurement make them relevant. They cannot be assumed negligible or imported wholesale. Their relevance is a model-selection question with a recorded boundary.

The register retains residual work for currently unclassified claims, uninstantiated physical maps, absent measurements, unknown preservation, untested historical alternatives and social interpretation beyond material constraints. A residual is an object to investigate, not proof of a new mechanism.

The completed product at this stage is a scoped decomposition, explicit bridge inventory and research programme. Mathematical compatibility, numerical agreement, measured material response and historical attribution remain separate completion tests. The atlas does not claim new physics, a universal chemical algebra or an established ancient alkali-quarrying technique.

## First research sequence

1. Fix one pyramid, component and question. Use Khufu's Great Pyramid as the first anchor and retain the identities of every comparison monument, quarry, specimen and record.
2. Assemble the material, structural, environmental and archaeological context before selecting the depth of physical or chemical calculation.
3. Choose an investigation lane and split its claims into the nine operation families. Keep observation, mechanism, process feasibility and historical occurrence separately typed.
4. Declare competing forward histories with explicit joint inputs, material and energy accounts, detector models and failure conditions.
5. Predict a withheld observable: a structural record, phase, altered layer, surface feature, removal rate or process resource requirement appropriate to the chosen question.
6. Carry the prediction through exposure, preservation, conservation and sampling before comparing it with ancient material.
7. Join independently justified chronology, provenance, resources and contextual evidence to compare histories. Retain unresolved alternatives and negative results.

For the granite–alkali branch, this sequence begins with a named granite specimen and a source-defined treatment claim. The branch earns a connection to the pyramid case through the resulting material and historical evidence.

The algebra's contribution is to preserve these bridges and prevent a successful step from silently authorizing a stronger one. The scientific payoff is a prediction about an unmeasured response or surviving trace that can distinguish histories.
